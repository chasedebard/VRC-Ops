2. Information we collect

2.1 Account and authentication information
When you create a VRC Ops account, we collect the email address you use to register.
VRC Ops currently supports email and password authentication and conditional multi-factor authentication using an authenticator application and time-based one-time passwords (TOTP).
Authentication is provided through Supabase Auth. VRC Ops does not store your password in plaintext.
Supabase may process authentication information necessary to authenticate your account, maintain sessions, perform password resets, verify accounts, enforce rate limits, and manage MFA factors.
We also ask for the month and year you were born, when you create an account and, for accounts created earlier, the first time you sign in on a version of the App that asks. We use it for one purpose: to check that you are at least 14 years old, which is the minimum age for VRC Ops (Section 13). We keep the month and year, not the day, in a private record. No league member can see it, and we do not use it for anything else. You cannot change it in the App once it is entered; if you made a mistake, contact us. If you enter a date that shows you are under 14 while creating an account, no account is created, we do not keep what you entered, and the App on that device will not take another answer for 90 calendar days (the device remembers only that a refusal happened).

2.2 Optional account profile information
You may optionally provide additional information for your VRC Ops profile, including your name or display name, Discord username or handle, PlayStation Network (PSN) identifier, and profile or driver image where supported.
Except for the email address required to maintain an account, these profile fields are optional.

2.3 Driver profiles and information about other people
VRC Ops separates a user account from a league's racing driver records.
A league Owner or Admin may create a driver record for someone who does not have a VRC Ops account. These records are intended to contain racing-related information and may include a driver or display name, racing number, driver photo, team, class, region, season participation and assignments, account-to-driver association if the driver later has an account, qualifying and race history, championship results and standings, and other in-game racing information used by the league.
League administrators cannot create a VRC Ops user account on another person's behalf through the driver-profile system. Driver records for people without accounts are not intended to contain account-level information such as email addresses, telephone numbers, birthdays, home addresses, or similar contact information.
Names, photographs, online identifiers, and similar information can still be personal information even when telephone numbers, addresses, or other contact details are not collected.
The league administrators who enter this information decide what goes into their league's records. If you provide information or a photograph relating to another person, you are responsible for having appropriate authority or permission to provide it to the league.
If a league holds information about you that you do not want it to hold, you can ask the league administrator or ask us (Section 11).

2.4 League, championship, season, event, and results information
VRC Ops stores information necessary to operate racing leagues, including information relating to leagues, championships, seasons, teams, classes, regions, tracks, scheduled events and race weekends, driver eligibility and participation, qualifying sessions, race sessions, starting or grid positions, finishing positions, lap counts, lap and sector times, total race times, gaps to the leader, DNS, DNF, and DSQ statuses, penalties, pole positions, fastest laps, bonus points, championship points, standings, historical driver performance, and other race-management information entered by authorized league members.
VRC Ops may calculate standings, scores, statistics, predictions, ratings, and other derived information from this data.

2.5 League access and permissions
League information is not publicly accessible without authentication.
A person must sign into a VRC Ops account and have permission to access a particular league.
Current league roles include Owner, Admin, Marshal, Driver, and Viewer.
The information and controls available to a user depend on their league membership and role.

2.6 Telemetry and Capture
VRC Ops includes an optional Capture feature for supported racing games and platforms, including supported Gran Turismo 7 telemetry.
When Capture is used, VRC Ops may communicate with a supported console or device on the user's local network.
The raw telemetry capture is processed locally and is not uploaded to our servers.
VRC Ops may create a parsed version of the telemetry and upload that parsed information to our servers. Depending on the available telemetry, parsed information may include information such as the game or simulation, vehicle, track, session information, lap information, sector information, lap or sector times, tire or fuel information, session classification, and other derived racing or telemetry measurements.
The console or PlayStation IP address entered or detected for Capture is stored locally on the device and is not stored by VRC Ops on our servers as league or account data.
Raw telemetry artifacts may remain locally on the user's device after a Capture session. These local artifacts are under the control of the device and are not stored by VRC Ops on its backend.
On Android, Capture runs as a foreground service and shows a notification while it is running, so the App asks for permission to show notifications. Finding a console on your local network uses the device's Wi-Fi multicast capability. Neither is used to find your location.

2.7 Predictions
VRC Ops provides race-prediction and forecasting functionality using data already available within VRC Ops.
The current prediction system is deterministic. It is not powered by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, or another external generative-AI or large-language-model provider.
Predictions may use relevant racing information such as historical results, championship data, driver performance, race participation, and other applicable league information.
Prediction outputs and evaluation history are not sent to an external AI provider.

2.8 Pit Wall and Race Engineer
Pit Wall and Race Engineer help you plan and review car setups.
Depending on what you use, VRC Ops stores on our servers, against your account, information such as the game, vehicle, track, and class you are working on, your setup values and changes, run cards and summaries of runs you choose to capture, driver feedback, the recommendations you were shown, and whether they helped.
This information is private to the account that created it. It is not shown to other members of your leagues, including league Owners and Admins.
Recommendations are produced by fixed rules and calculations within VRC Ops. They are not produced by an external generative-AI service.
Raw telemetry stays on your device (Section 2.6).

2.9 Global rating and awards
VRC Ops can calculate a rating for your account from official results in leagues where a league Owner or Admin has linked your account to a driver record. The rating belongs to your account, not to a single league's driver record.
The rating is off by default. No rating is calculated for your account, and none of your results are counted toward a rating, until you choose to turn it on. You turn it on in Settings. To do so, two-factor authentication must be switched on for your account. Results from races that begin at least 24 hours after you turn it on are counted. You can turn it off at any time in Settings. Turning it off ends your current rating period and stops new results being counted. If you later turn it on again, you start a new rating period.
We keep a record of rating changes, corrections, and your decisions to turn the rating on or off, so that a rating can be explained and corrected.
Ratings are calculated from official results by fixed rules. Results can be corrected or voided by the league, and ratings may be recalculated when that happens.
Today, no league member can see anyone's rating: the shared display has not started, and the App says it is coming soon. When it starts, other members of a league you belong to will see, next to your driver record, and only if you have turned the rating on and at least one race has counted: your tier (or "provisional" while your rating is still being established), your global rank once it is no longer provisional, a World Champion or National Champion badge if you hold one, and the finalized rating change for a race.
If you finish a quarter as the World Champion, you may be offered a free subscription period. To offer it, VRC Ops asks Apple's App Store Connect service to create a single-use offer code for the subscription product you hold. The code is kept against your account until redeemed, and we may email you about it. Only accounts that have turned the rating on can be selected.

An invitation email is sent only because a league Owner or Admin asked us to send it to that address. It contains the invitation and nothing promotional.

9. Data retention

We generally retain account and league information for as long as it is necessary to operate the applicable account, league, subscription, or VRC Ops functionality. The specific rules are:
Account profile and sign-in details: until you delete your account.
Month and year of birth: until you delete your account.
Memberships and invitations that belong to your account: removed when you delete your account or leave the league.
Consent and agreement records: removed with your account.
Private Pit Wall data and capture files: removed when you delete them or delete your account.
League records (championships, seasons, events, results, standings, rating history, and driver records): kept for as long as the league exists, and kept after a league is closed, as described in Section 10.
Records of your communications with us about privacy requests: kept in the VRC Ops email account for as long as reasonably needed to handle the request and to show how it was handled.
Other records, namely unaccepted invitations, subscription and entitlement records, rating audit records, account-deletion request records, and provider logs: we do not run our own automatic deletion schedule for these today. They are kept in our database for as long as the account or league they belong to exists, and deleting an account (Section 10) removes or de-identifies them as described there. Logs and delivery records held by Supabase, Resend, and Apple are kept under those providers' standard retention.
Information may also be retained where reasonably necessary for security, fraud prevention, troubleshooting, legal compliance, dispute resolution, transaction records, or technical recovery.
Certain logs, transactional-email records, backup copies, or service-provider records may remain for a limited period after information is deleted from active VRC Ops systems. Where applicable, such residual information may remain for as long as the service provider's standard backup and infrastructure-retention schedule provides, unless a shorter or longer period is required by law.
Information remaining only in backup or recovery systems will not be intentionally restored for ordinary VRC Ops use except where necessary for legitimate disaster recovery or system restoration.
Raw telemetry Capture artifacts stored only on your device are not included in VRC Ops server-side retention because VRC Ops does not possess those raw files.
VRC Ops may suspend or revoke access to the service where appropriate under its Terms of Service.

10. Account deletion

VRC Ops provides an in-app account deletion process. You can find it in Settings. You may also ask for help by contacting debard.chase@outlook.com.
When an account is deleted, the VRC Ops authentication account is deleted, account-level profile information associated with that account is deleted, your avatar and personal settings are deleted, your consent and agreement records are deleted, your private Pit Wall data and capture files are deleted, and your memberships and invitations are removed.

Driver records after account deletion
A VRC Ops account and a league driver record are separate records.
If your account was linked to a driver record, deleting your account unlinks that record. The driver's photo, biography, and platform and racing identifiers are removed from it, and your account is no longer connected to it.
The record itself stays in the league, so the league's history is not lost. Its name is replaced: the driver's name becomes the word "Driver" followed by the driver's racing number. For example, if the racing number was 73, the driver becomes "Driver 73". If the record has no racing number, it becomes "Driver". The first and last names are removed too.
A league may retain racing records necessary to preserve legitimate league history, including the racing number, race and qualifying results, championship history, points and standings, and rating history. A retained driver record is not an active login account.
If a driver record was never linked to an account, the person it describes can ask the league administrator or ask us to replace the name in the same way, while keeping the league's results intact.

League Owners
If other active members remain in a league you own, you must transfer ownership to one of them before your account can be deleted. VRC Ops does not transfer ownership automatically, and subscription or billing arrangements do not move with it.
If you are the only active member of a league you own, deleting your account closes the league. A closed league has no owner, cannot be joined or changed, and is not visible to anyone, but VRC Ops keeps its racing records (championships, seasons, events, results, standings, and driver records) so that the history is not lost. Personal information attached to your account is removed or unlinked from the retained records. A league that contains only unused drafts and no racing history is deleted instead.
Backups and logs are described in Section 9.

19. If you are in Australia

19.1 Our approach
We handle the personal information of people in Australia in line with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles, to the extent they apply to us.
We collect your information to run your account and your leagues, as described in Sections 2 and 3. If you do not give us the information we need (for example, your email address), we cannot create your account.

19.2 Overseas disclosure
We are likely to disclose your personal information to recipients outside Australia. They are our service providers in the United States and, depending on the provider, in other countries (Sections 5 and 6).

19.3 Access, correction, and complaints
You can ask for access to your personal information or ask us to correct it (Section 11). We reply within 30 days.
If you want to complain, write to us first (Section 14). If you are not satisfied with our reply, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au).

19.4 Automated processing
Standings, predictions, ratings, and Pit Wall recommendations use automated calculations (Section 3). See Section 3 for what they do and do not decide.

19.5 Data breaches
If a data breach involving your personal information is likely to result in serious harm to you, we will notify you and the regulator as the law requires.
